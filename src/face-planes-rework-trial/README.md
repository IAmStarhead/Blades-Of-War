# Orbital and cheek plane rework — isolated trial

Open `/src/face-planes-rework-trial/` via Vite. Left reconstructs the prior `face-final-review-trial` right-hand figure. Right is the rework; no original game assets or earlier studies are changed. Male and female are shown separately in front, three-quarter, both profiles, untextured clay, opposite light, fixed head poses and body scale.

The prior study stacked several procedural bulges on the game head. This test estimates and subtracts their contributions to brows, sockets and cheeks (including the original game-head brow and cheek), then adds a lower, curved brow rim / under-brow recess and wider cheek transition. This is an approximation: original deformations were applied through several prior operations, so subtracting their formulas does not perfectly undo their visual effect. Nose and mouth area have a soft exclusion, not absolute preservation. The head mesh is split into four triangles per original triangle without moving the initial surface, then the new face displacement is applied; normal adjustments are blended relative to the pre-displacement geometric normals to avoid lighting revealing the underlying triangulation. The test is not a production mesh: it is denser and the topology was not redesigned for animation.

Female lip artwork is narrowed locally by an inverse UV warp, keeping its existing height and shape as much as the feathered warp allows; this does not sculpt an anatomically separate mouth. Eyes, ear shapes and the old, separate neck arrangement are retained; the neck seam is not repaired. Brow shadow can still appear above the brow depending on lighting and pre-existing texture/geometry. The result is a visual experiment, not proof that anatomy and artistic quality are resolved.

Reference rationale: the brow ridge bounds the top of the orbit, and its underside can turn away from light; cheekbone/side planes extend outward from beneath the eye rather than being isolated spherical bumps. See https://lifedrawing.academy/drawing-lessons/skull-drawing-easy and https://lifedrawing.academy/drawing-lessons/head-and-face-proportions. These are explanatory references, not assets copied into the repository.

`node tests/face-planes-rework-trial.mjs` tests synthetic subdivision, bounded/symmetric finite displacement, and original isolation. `npm run verify`: 232/232. Neither confirms artistic quality, exact eye-to-brow alignment, neck continuity, or production/animation performance. Ears in the earlier trial derive from CC0 reference meshes (see `../head-reference/README.md`).
